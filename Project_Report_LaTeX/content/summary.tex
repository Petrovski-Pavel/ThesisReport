This report addresses the problem of autonomous service-robot interaction with
elevator panels in hospital-like environments. For reliable multi-floor
navigation, the robot must detect elevator buttons, interpret their labels, and
estimate their spatial position under varying lighting conditions, viewpoints,
and hardware constraints.

To address this problem, the project develops a modular perception pipeline that
combines instance segmentation, optical character recognition, depth-based
target localization, and deployment-oriented model optimization. The workflow
also includes an Active Data Collector to support environment-specific data
collection and later model improvement after deployment.

The results show that the baseline YOLOv8s segmentation model remained the most
reliable choice on the held-out original test set, reaching a mask
$\mathrm{mAP}_{50\text{:}95}$ of 0.58295 at 640-pixel evaluation. In the target
university deployment environment, however, the best segmentation result was
obtained after fine-tuning the YOLO model with environment-specific data
collected through the ADC node, which increased mask
$\mathrm{mAP}_{50\text{:}95}$ on the HAN Building R29 test split from 0.12290 to
0.50389. OCR fine-tuning also improved button-label recognition on cropped
button images. After TensorRT optimization and ROS integration on the NVIDIA
Jetson Orin Nano, the final runtime pipeline achieved about 13.1 frames per
second, corresponding to approximately 76 milliseconds per frame. With the
collector enabled, the system still operated at about 10 frames per second, or
roughly 100 milliseconds per frame.

These results show that practical embedded perception for elevator-button
interaction is feasible with a modular computer-vision workflow. The project
therefore provides a concrete basis for future full-system elevator interaction
and for continued improvement through deployment-time data collection and
retraining.
