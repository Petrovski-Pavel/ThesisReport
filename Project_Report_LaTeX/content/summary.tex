This report investigates how a service robot can reliably perceive elevator
panels in hospital-like environments. For autonomous multi-floor operation, the
robot must detect individual elevator buttons, interpret their labels, and
estimate their spatial position under varying lighting conditions, viewpoints,
and embedded-hardware constraints.

To address this problem, the project develops a modular computer-vision
workflow that combines instance segmentation for button localization, optical
character recognition for semantic interpretation, and depth-based target
estimation for downstream robot use. The workflow also includes
deployment-time data collection and retraining support so that the perception
system can be adapted to new environments after field use.

The results show that the baseline YOLOv8s segmentation model remained the
strongest general detector on the original held-out test set, achieving a mask
mAP50:95 of 0.58295 at the selected 640-pixel deployment setting. In the
target deployment environment, fine-tuning with environment-specific data from
HAN Building R29 increased mask mAP50:95 from 0.12290 to 0.50463, showing that
a small amount of deployment-specific data can substantially improve
target-domain performance without degrading the original-domain result. The OCR
tuning stage also improved button-label recognition quality on held-out button
crops.

After optimization and integration on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, the final
perception pipeline operated at about 13.1 frames per second, while still
maintaining about 10 frames per second when the data-collection component was
active. Reviewed deployment results further indicate that the system was most
reliable at distances up to approximately 1 m, with detection confidence
decreasing as the distance to the target button increased. Overall, the project
demonstrates that reliable embedded perception for elevator-button interaction
is feasible with a modular workflow that combines detection, recognition,
deployment-oriented optimization, and structured field-data collection for
continued improvement.
